# VLM/Multimodal Inference Optimizations — 2026 Support Catalog (vLLM / SGLang)

Context assumed: 4x RTX 5090 (SM120, 32GB, PCIe-only), vLLM ≥0.17 + FlashInfer + PyTorch ≥2.9; ~4464x2160 screenshots (1K–9.4K vision tokens), shared few-shot prefix, short constrained-JSON outputs, batch + interactive endpoint.

---

## 1. Visual token compression / pruning / merging

**Bottom line: as of July 2026, NO training-free pruning method (FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip, PyramidDrop, ToMe, LLaVA-PruMerge, DART) is natively supported in vLLM or SGLang for images.** The only engine-native pruning is **video-only EVS** in vLLM.

- **vLLM RFC #45098 "Image Token Pruning for Multimodal Models"** (opened **June 10, 2026**, still open, no linked PRs) proposes generic `--image-pruning-rate` / `--image-pruning-config` CLI args and a pluggable dispatch, modeled on the existing EVS integration. Watch this — it's the first credible path to engine-native image pruning, but it's RFC-stage only (github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/45098, Jun 2026).
- **EVS (Efficient Video Sampling, NVIDIA)** — IS merged in vLLM: runtime-only, prunes temporally-static video patches after ViT+adapter, before the LLM; up to 4x TTFT reduction on video (arXiv 2510.14624; vLLM Nemotron blog, Oct 31 2025). Works with Qwen3-VL video inputs (bug #30847 shows active use, with a timestamp/token-count alignment bug for pruned video). **Irrelevant to your single-image frames unless you batch frames as pseudo-video (see §4).** Note: enabling EVS disables video ViT CUDA graphs.
- **SGLang**: no native visual token pruning/merging feature merged as of mid-2026; its multimodal efficiency work went into EPD + embedding caching instead (LMSYS EPD blog, Jan 12 2026).
- **Research pipeline (custom-integration territory only)**: FastV (prune after layer 2 by attention), SparseVLM/SparseVLM+ (text-guided progressive pruning), VisionZip (encoder self-attention dominant-token selection + merging), plus 2025–26 successors (LearnPruner, Script, AsymVLM, N"uwa — arXiv 2604.23950, 2512.01949, 2605.29535, 2602.02951). All require forking model code; most were validated on LLaVA-class models and **transfer poorly to Qwen3-VL's DeepStack multi-layer injection** (pruning indices must be kept consistent across the 3 injected LLM layers). Quality risk on fine-grained debug-screenshot text/HUD reading is HIGH (pruning methods notoriously drop small text).
- **Model-side compression (the practical route in 2026)**:
  - **InternVL3.5-Flash**: Visual Resolution Router (ViR) routes each patch to 256→64-token pixel-shuffle by semantic richness; ~50% visual-token reduction at "nearly full performance"; requires the ViCO-trained Flash checkpoints (InternVL3.5 blog, Aug 26 2025; HF OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-*-Flash). It's a different checkpoint, not a serve flag.
  - **Qwen3-VL / Qwen3.5 DeepStack**: injects multi-level ViT features into the first ~3 LLM layers via residuals — improves quality **without adding tokens**; it is NOT a token reducer. The token reducer in Qwen is the standard 2x2 merger (arXiv 2511.21631, Nov 2025).
  - **Ovis 2.5**: native-resolution ViT + structural embedding; no runtime pruning knob.
- **Verdict**: for your stack, don't chase pruning papers. Get the same FLOPs reduction safely via `max_pixels` budgeting (§4), which is exact, supported, and cache-friendly. Re-check RFC #45098 in Q4 2026.

## 2. Vision encoder optimizations

- **FP8 on the vision tower**: llm-compressor (≥0.4.0, multimodal support) **deliberately skips the vision tower** — examples use `ignore=["re:vision_tower.*", "re:multi_modal_projector.*", "lm_head"]`; official RedHatAI/AngelSlim/Qwen FP8 VLM checkpoints keep ViT+projector in BF16 because compressing them "offers little benefit with respect to performance-accuracy tradeoff" (LLM Compressor multimodal_vision docs; Red Hat Developer, Feb 19 2025). vLLM runs the ViT in BF16 even when the LM is FP8/NVFP4. **Do not attempt ViT FP8 yourself** — encoder is compute-bound but small; the win is in the LM. Verdict: use official FP8 checkpoints (LM-only quantized); mature.
- **ViT CUDA graphs — NEW and directly relevant to you**: vLLM `compilation_config={"cudagraph_mm_encoder": true}` captures the full encoder forward per token-budget level; supported for **Qwen3-VL, InternVL, GLM-4.1V lineage, Llama4, DeepSeek-OCR**; benchmarked **on Blackwell** with FLASH_ATTN/FLASHINFER ViT backends: 11–19% mean / up to 40% P99 encoder latency reduction single-GPU, 18–44% mean (85% P99 with FlashInfer) at 4x TP/DP (vLLM docs "Vision Encoder (ViT) CUDA Graphs", 2026). Tunables: `encoder_cudagraph_token_budgets`, `encoder_cudagraph_max_vision_items_per_batch`. Quality risk: none (numerics unchanged). Verdict: **enable it**; maturity medium-high (recent but tested on your exact GPU class).
- **torch.compile of the encoder**: `compile_mm_encoder` in CompilationConfig; supported for Qwen-VL-family and Llama4; ~3% throughput on Hopper reported, one-time ~13s extra compile cached to `~/.cache/vllm/torch_compile_cache` (vLLM docs "torch.compile with Multimodal Encoders"; dev.to M. Ketkar). Caveat: compile + encoder CUDA graphs together is "unspecified behavior" per docs. Verdict: minor win; enable if stable, prefer CUDA graphs.
- **Encoder parallelism on PCIe boxes — your biggest encoder lever**: `--mm-encoder-tp-mode data` replaces per-layer all-reduce (58–126 collectives/forward under weight-TP) with one all-gather, running the whole ViT per-rank on different images (batch-level DP). 10–45% throughput gain reported; the gain is LARGER on your no-P2P PCIe topology where all-reduce is punishingly slow. Supported for Qwen2.5/3-VL, InternVL, GLM-V, etc. (`supports_encoder_tp_mode=True`) (vLLM Optimization docs; PR #23909). Verdict: **mandatory flag for you**; mature.
- **EPD (encoder–prefill–decode disaggregation)**:
  - **vLLM**: native EPD merged Nov 2025 (PR #25233), shipped **since v0.11.1**: proxy/router + remote embedding storage + EC connectors (`ECConnectorBase` etc.); on 4xA100 with Qwen3-VL-4B, ~2x goodput and 30–50% P99 TTFT reduction at 4 images/request (vLLM blog "Encoder Disaggregation", Dec 15 2025).
  - **SGLang**: EPD productionized Jan 2026: `--encoder-only --enable-prefix-mm-cache` encoder tier, `--disaggregation-mode prefill --language-only --encoder-urls ...`, ZMQ or Mooncake transfer; 6–8x TTFT, ~2x throughput on Qwen3-VL-235B — but using **50% extra GPUs**, and explicitly "less efficient or even counterproductive" for image-light (1-image) requests (LMSYS blog, Jan 12 2026; RFC #24945).
  - Verdict for you: **skip EPD**. It pays off for multi-image (4–8/request) fleets with spare GPUs; on a single 4x5090 node with 1 image/request, colocated + `mm-encoder-tp-mode data` + ViT CUDA graphs wins. Consider only if you later split interactive (decode) from batch (encode-heavy) across nodes.

## 3. Multimodal caching

- **Processor cache (CPU-side)**: vLLM `--mm-processor-cache-gb` (default 4 GiB), `--mm-processor-cache-type lru|shm` (`shm` recommended for TP>1 / multiple API procs). Caches HF-processor outputs (pixel_values) keyed by content hash (vLLM Optimization docs). **Workload note**: a 9.4K-token 4464x2160 frame produces ~80–90MB of pixel_values, so 4GiB holds only ~45 unique frames — for thousands of unique frames per batch run this cache thrashes uselessly; set `--mm-processor-cache-gb 0` for the batch endpoint and keep it (shm) on the interactive endpoint where users re-query the same screenshot. A Workable SRE report (Medium, 2025) also documents memory-leak-like growth from oversized mm caches on long-running servers.
- **Prefix caching with images**: vLLM V1 folds a **content hash of each image** ("mm_hash") into block hashes, so identical image + identical prefix = KV reuse; default hash **sha256 since v0.11** (collision-safe; `xxhash` optional but documented as risky in multi-tenant settings) (vLLM Prefix Caching design doc). SGLang does the equivalent in RadixAttention (image SHA-256 as radix key). Correctness caveats: (a) hashes are computed on raw media + processor kwargs — a different per-request `max_pixels` yields different tokens, no false hits; (b) hybrid-attention models: **Qwen3.5's GDN layers force a 528-token block granularity, so prompts <528 tokens get 0% prefix hit and hit rates quantize coarsely** (vLLM issue #40696, 2026) — your few-shot prefix is long, so fine, but pad-aware prompt design helps.
- **Cross-request identical-image reuse**: three tiers — (1) processor cache (skips CPU preprocessing), (2) encoder-output/embedding cache (skips ViT: vLLM encoder cache within a step + EPD remote embedding cache; SGLang `--enable-prefix-mm-cache` + `SGLANG_VLM_CACHE_SIZE_MB`, default 4GB), (3) KV prefix cache (skips prefill). For your **shared few-shot prefix with fixed exemplar images**: all three hit every request — put the exemplar images at the *front* of the prompt and keep byte-identical files so hashes match. LMCache extends multimodal KV offload/reuse to CPU/disk if you need cross-restart persistence (LMCache blog, Jul 3 2025; supports Qwen3.5 hybrid models per docs.lmcache.ai).
- **Precomputed embeddings input**: vLLM accepts `image_embeds` (+ `image_grid_thw` for Qwen-VL) instead of pixels — you can encode once offline and serve LM-only (`--language-model-only` exists for Qwen3.5 recipe). Rough edges reported for Qwen3-VL/InternVL 3D-vs-2D tensor shapes (issues #32309, #26749). Verdict: viable for re-running the same frame corpus against evolving prompts; medium maturity.

## 4. Resolution / token budgeting — your #1 lever

- **Per-request `mm_processor_kwargs`**: vLLM supports `{"max_pixels": ..., "min_pixels": ...}` (or `resized_height/width`) per request for Qwen-VL family (PR #9612; forum-confirmed; occasional model-specific "ignored kwarg" regressions, e.g. issue #15364 — test on your version). This enables a **max_pixels ladder**: run the fleet at a cheap tier (e.g. ~1280x768 ≈ 1.3K tokens), re-run only low-confidence frames at high tier (native ≈ 9.4K tokens). 4464x2160 at full res is ~7x the tokens of a 1.5MP resize — on a compute-bound prefill workload this is a near-linear 5–7x throughput swing, far more than any pruning paper delivers.
- **Engine-side dynamic/task-aware resolution**: nothing landed in vLLM/SGLang that auto-picks resolution per task; the only adaptive mechanisms are model-side (InternVL3.5-Flash ViR patch router; Qwen "smart_resize" bounds). Qwen3.5 recipe exposes visual context control via `--mm-processor-kwargs '{"longest_edge": ...}'`-style keys (vLLM recipes "Qwen3.5 Usage Guide", 2026). Build the ladder in your client.
- **Video vs multi-image mode**: Qwen-VL-family video mode merges 2 temporal frames per token group (temporal_patch_size=2) and adds timestamp tokens → ~half the tokens per frame vs sending the same frames as separate images, and it unlocks EVS pruning in vLLM (§1). For temporally-adjacent ADAS debug frames, batching N consecutive frames as one "video" request + EVS is a legitimate cost hack **if** your task tolerates joint answers; per-frame JSON verdicts argue for single-image requests.
- **`limit-mm-per-prompt` size hints**: vLLM accepts `{"image": {"count": 1, "width": 4464, "height": 2160}}`-style hints so memory profiling reserves for YOUR media instead of model maxima → more KV cache headroom on 32GB cards; `--skip-mm-profiling` also exists (faster startup, you own the OOM risk) (vLLM Optimization docs; PR #25810). Also set `--max-num-batched-tokens` ≥ your largest single-image token count + slack — a multimodal item can't be chunked across scheduler steps.

## 5. Multimodal speculative decoding (2026 status)

- **EAGLE/EAGLE-3 for VLMs in vLLM**: merged (PR #22872, Qwen2.5-VL enablement; "multimodal support with warning" in release notes). **AngelSlim ships ready EAGLE-3 drafts for Qwen3-VL: 2B/4B/30B-A3B-Instruct** (`AngelSlim/Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct_eagle3` on HF), benchmarked on vLLM v0.12: accept length 1.8–3.5, **1.4–1.9x speedup** at num_speculative_tokens 2–4; the AngelSlim paper (arXiv 2602.21233, Feb 2026) claims 1.8–2.0x across its models. No public EAGLE-3 draft for Qwen3-VL-32B (dense) or GLM-4.6V spotted — would need SpecForge/AngelSlim training. Independent benchmarking (MMSpec, arXiv 2603.14989) warns VLM speedups are often more modest than text-only (one Qwen2.5-VL-7B config measured ~0.96x, i.e. a loss) — **benchmark on your traffic before committing**.
- **SGLang**: EAGLE-3 for Qwen2.5-VL works; **Qwen3-VL-MoE + EAGLE3 crashed as of sglang v0.5.7** (missing `set_eagle3_layers_to_capture`, issue #17935); SpecForge added Qwen3-VL-8B draft configs (PR #542). Maturity behind vLLM for VLM drafts.
- **MTP in multimodal checkpoints**: **Qwen3.5 ships native MTP** — vLLM recipe: `--speculative-config '{"method":"mtp","num_speculative_tokens":1}'`; recommended for low-concurrency/latency, explicitly **degrades throughput under high concurrency** (KV consumed by speculative tokens) → use on the interactive endpoint, not the batch run (vLLM recipes Qwen3.5, 2026). GLM-4.6/4.6V: GLM-4.6 MTP had open vLLM issues (#25993) with later "GLM MTP post-final-norm" fixes in releases; treat GLM-4.6V MTP-in-vLLM as "verify on your build".
- **N-gram / prompt-lookup — best fit for your JSON workload**: `{"method":"ngram","num_speculative_tokens":3-5,"prompt_lookup_max":...,"prompt_lookup_min":...}`; free (no draft model), and constrained JSON with repetitive keys is the ideal case. **Structured outputs + spec decode compose in vLLM V1**, but a known 2026 bug: with `prompt_lookup_min=2` (default) Qwen3-class + structured/tool output can corrupt — set **`prompt_lookup_min≥8`** (vLLM issue #40875). With ~200–500 output tokens against 3–15K-token prompts your run is prefill-dominated anyway — expect maybe 1.1–1.3x end-to-end from any spec decode; prioritize §2/§4 first.

## 6. Other VLM-specific notes for this deployment

- **Model support snapshot (verify at deploy)**: Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (released Feb 24 2026, Apache-2.0, multimodal MoE, 256 experts, GDN hybrid + MTP; official FP8 checkpoint exists) is a first-class vLLM citizen with a published recipe; GLM-4.6V (106B, Dec 9 2025) + GLM-4.6V-Flash (9B) supported in vLLM/SGLang GLM-V path; InternVL3.5-Flash variants supported via InternVL path (verify ViR routing works in vLLM — it's processor-side, generally does).
- **Recommended 4x5090 flag stack (batch endpoint)**: `-tp 4` (or TP2+PP2 if PCIe all-reduce hurts; measure), `--mm-encoder-tp-mode data`, `--enable-prefix-caching`, `compilation_config '{"cudagraph_mm_encoder": true}'`, `--limit-mm-per-prompt` with exact size hints, `--mm-processor-cache-gb 0` (batch) / `--mm-processor-cache-type shm` (interactive), ngram spec-decode with `prompt_lookup_min≥8` on the QA endpoint only. For Qwen3.5: watch `--max-cudagraph-capture-size` vs mamba cache error (known issue in recipe) and the 528-token prefix-cache granularity.
- **ViT attention backend**: ViT CUDA-graph benchmarks show FLASHINFER giving the best P99 on Blackwell multi-GPU; the mm-encoder attention backend is selectable independently of the LM backend in 2026 vLLM builds.
- **Multi-image batching**: `--limit-mm-per-prompt '{"image": N}'` raises per-request image caps but inflates memory profiling and encoder-cache pressure; for 1-image requests keep it at 1 (plus few-shot exemplar count) to maximize KV cache on 32GB.
- **Interleaved few-shot prompts**: keep exemplar images byte-identical and position-stable so mm-hash prefix caching holds; any change to `mm_processor_kwargs` on the shared prefix invalidates all cached blocks downstream.
- **Research to watch (not deployable)**: VLCache ("compute 2% of vision tokens, reuse 98%", arXiv 2512.12977), SpecVLM (spec decode with pruned-vision drafts, arXiv 2509.11815), ElasticMM (elastic multimodal parallelism, arXiv 2507.10069), vLLM RFC #45098 (native image pruning).

**Priority order for your workload**: (1) max_pixels ladder, (2) `--mm-encoder-tp-mode data` + ViT CUDA graphs, (3) prefix caching hygiene for the few-shot prefix, (4) FP8 LM-only official checkpoints, (5) ngram spec decode on QA endpoint, (6) EAGLE-3 only if you pick Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (draft exists), (7) skip EPD and pruning papers.
